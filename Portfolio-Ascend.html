Overview

Bouldering is one of my hobbies, whether it be climbing plastic rocks in a gym or going outoors and finding some real rocks to play with.

In climbing gyms, its hard to know what routes are available, unavailable, due for a reset or what type of project they are.

There are exisiting apps out in the market, however I believe there is an opportunity to provide a better app through creating a focused experience that solves for these issues.

Architecture Understanding the Problem

The problem is lack of communication, I have identified that without a dedicated platform, these key concepts would be an arduous process to execute conistently.

  1. Projects/Routes easily available for climbers to see.
  2. Route Setters to easily upload route to a platform.
  3. Gym Owners to manage the account

I also Identified three different users to design for, 'Gym Owner', 'Route Setter' and 'Climber'. All with a different needs.

Firstly I looked at the Competition, analysed who their target user is, what their product purpose is and their overall UX structure.

Secondly I created 2 'Archetypes' Archetype #1 | Route Setter / Owner and Archetype #2 | Climber, 'Gym owner' and 'Route Setter' had similar needs therefore to simplify this project, i combined them into one artifact.

Afterwards I created empathy maps based on the 'Archetypes' to understand these users actions, behaviour and thoughts.

  1. Emapthy Map #1 | Route Setter / Owner
  2. Emapthy Map #2 | Climber

RocketDeveloping a Solution

With rearch artifacts established, we can now start to ideate and develop a solution.

I created Card Sorting #1 and Card Sorting #2 to group features for the product in a order of importance.

Next was to create a visual map of these flows User Flow #1 and User Flow #2 to define paths users can take, serving as a blueprint for creating the final user experience (UX).

  2. Customer Journey Map - Climber

Wall_ArtCompany Brand Aesthetics

This is more in-line with graphic design than traditional UX or UI, but creating an intuitive product is one thing, creating a brand, a feeling for those users to attach to is another. So in establishing a brand I had to:

  1. Create a brief that would outline what the brand is about.
  2. Mock up a moodboard to capture the vision for the brand.
  3. Establish brand logo and colour pallet
  4. Ensure they're WCAG compliant
  5. Adapt the orginal screens to match the brand guidelines.

There they can filter the specific gyms if necessary, but this will allow them to be notified of any new routes, as well as updates pertaining to business hours or events. Transparency and accessibility being the primary focus for this function.

Here you can navigate and see what climbs are available, swap to whichever gym you are visiting.

Navigate the environment without too much graphical elements bombarding users with information that is not important to them nor provides much value to justify.

The profile consists of:
(1) Projects - List of all your climbs you are watching or have completed/
(2) Save Post - Information you want to remember and keep in your loop.
(3) Metrics - information to keep you on track and see how well you are doing.

ReviewsRoute Metrics / Reviews

Having information about the climb was another essential part of this product, having the ability to see what others have said or felt the climb is like, providing transparency to those who want to know; also allows route setters to see how their project is being perceived.

DashboardProfile Metrics

key indicators to help keep track of how many climbs you have accomplished during your bouldering years, these add some key figures to allow users to track what they have accomplished. However not going too over the top and drowing them in statistics and data which can be overwhelming.

SettingsSettings Section

This isn't as glamerous compared to the other areas of this project, but necessary depending on how YOU want the project to build / scale over time, this contributes to the biggest pain-points in any software / app where users have to configure basic to more advance settings.

Laptop_MacLaptop UX

The laptop (Also applies to desktop setups too) is for front of house where most staff reside. whether they are welcoming in climbers and checking up in the system, that is the reason for having an experience for this aspect ratio.

TabletTablet UX

This is not in all gyms admitingly, but I have seen many (Australia and United kingdom) utalise tablets, either for quick sign ups or signing any OHS forms. However this experience would also match for Route setters too in the situation that gyms have the technology available for them to use.

MobileMobile UX

This is the primary experience for majority of users, this device was the most important to get right, which is why i designed with the mobile experience first then build up from there. This is common practice for apps as designing with limited screen architecture imposes restrictions which means more difficulty from a design and development standpoint.

Conclusion

This project was more difficult, considering this involved creating flows for regular day to day tasks plus onboarding, the latter which did change elements of the flow, however would increase adoption rates due to the quick and easy method of signing up.

This project in particular was more technical due to what flows were accounted for, and designing for a mobile experience does tend to add more difficult due to the 'limited' screen infrastructure available.

However this product was successful when pitching to the various users recruited for this experience. Solved the problems identified and branding for the product was well received.
